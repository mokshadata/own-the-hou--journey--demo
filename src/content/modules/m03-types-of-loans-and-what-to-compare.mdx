Comparing the factors below between lenders is important to help save you money over the life of your loan. 

* **Interest Rate**. Comparing interest rates is very important. A slightly lower interest rate – for example, one-quarter of a percent lower (0.25%) – can reduce the total amount you pay in interest by thousands of dollars over the time period of your loan. Be aware that interest rates change over time, so a rate provided early in the process may not be your final rate. However, lenders that can offer lower interest rates earlier in the process may also be able to offer lower rates at the time of closing.
* **Credit Requirements**. Some lenders require higher credit scores than others. A credit score of 620 is usually the minimum for a conventional loan, but FHA loans can be available with a credit score of 580 and above depending on other criteria that your lender will examine. If your credit is below 580, see our tips to improve your credit so that you can become more mortgage-eligible.
* **Fees**. Lenders charge “origination charges,” or fees to issue your loan, as part of your closing costs. You can ask lenders how they calculate these “origination charges” and the range of fees you can expect. You can also find the final charges on your Loan Estimate form on page 2, Section D, Total Loan Costs.
* **Down Payment Assistance Approved Lenders**. If you plan to use down payment assistance (DPA), not every lender can work with every program. Many DPA programs require you to use a qualified or approved lender. Before choosing a lender, confirm that they are approved for the assistance programs you may be eligible for.

You can compare these factors by asking questions before you provide any financial documents to a lender. Once you provide all of your financial documentation and work through underwriting, your lender will provide you a Loan Estimate that confirms the terms of your loan, including the interest rate, the required down payment, and more. You can read more about the Loan Estimate form and what the various parts of it mean on the U.S. [Consumer Protection Financial Bureau’s website](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/compare/compare-loan-estimates/).
