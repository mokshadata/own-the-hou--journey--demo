Comparing the factors below between <Term client:load term="lenders">lenders</Term> is important to help save you money over the life of your loan. 

* <Term client:load term="interest rate">**Interest Rate**</Term>. Comparing interest rates is very important. A slightly lower interest rate – for example, one-quarter of a percent lower (0.25%) – can reduce the total amount you pay in interest by thousands of dollars over the time period of your loan.

  Be aware that interest rates change over time, so a rate provided early in the process may not be your final rate. However, lenders that can offer lower interest rates earlier in the process may also be able to offer lower rates at the time of closing.
* **Credit Requirements**. Some lenders require higher credit scores than others. A <Term client:load term="credit score">credit score</Term> of 620 is usually the minimum for a conventional loan, but <Term client:load term="FHA loans">FHA loans</Term> can be available with a credit score of 580 and above depending on other criteria that your lender will examine. If your credit is below 580, see [our tips to improve your credit](/own-the-hou--journey--demo/chapters/c02-what-s-your-budget/s04-understanding-your-credit/m02-resources-to-improve-your-credit/) so that you can become more mortgage-eligible.
* **Fees**. Lenders charge “<Term client:load term="origination charges" mode="definer">**origination charges**</Term>,” or fees to issue your loan, as part of your <Term client:load term="closing costs">closing costs</Term>. You can ask lenders how they calculate these “origination charges” and the range of fees you can expect. You can also find the final charges on your <Term client:load term="loan estimate form">Loan Estimate form</Term> on page 2, Section D, Total Loan Costs.
* **Down Payment Assistance Approved Lenders**. If you plan to use <Term client:load term="down payment assistance">down payment assistance (DPA)</Term>, not every lender can work with every program. Many DPA programs require you to use a qualified or approved lender. Before choosing a lender, confirm that they are approved for the assistance programs you may be eligible for.

You can compare these factors by asking questions before you provide any financial documents to a lender. Once you provide all of your financial documentation and work through underwriting, your lender will provide you a <Term client:load term="loan estimate form" mode="definer">**Loan Estimate form**</Term> that confirms the terms of your loan, including the <Term client:load term="interest rate">interest rate</Term>, the required down payment, and more. You can read more about the Loan Estimate form and what the various parts of it mean on the U.S. [Consumer Protection Financial Bureau’s website](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/compare/compare-loan-estimates/).
