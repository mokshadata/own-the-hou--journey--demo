Once your offer is accepted, one of the most important next steps is hiring a home inspector. A <Term term="home-inspector" mode="definer">**home inspector**</Term> conducts an inspection to examine major systems and components of the house, such as the roof, foundation, plumbing, electrical systems, heating and cooling, and appliances. They look for safety issues, signs of damage, and potential repairs you may need to plan for. The goal isn’t to find a “perfect” home — it’s to help you understand what you’re buying. 

The inspection is typically completed during the first 5-7 days after the offer, during the “option period.” After the inspection, you’ll receive a written <Term term="inspection-report" mode="definer">**inspection report**</Term>. If issues come up, you may be able to negotiate repairs, request <Term term="credits" mode="definer">**credits**</Term>, or decide whether to move forward with the purchase.

### Upfront Cost: Paying for the Inspection

You, as the homebuyer, are responsible for paying for the inspection – this is an upfront cost you need to plan for, and it is paid at the time of hire. Prices vary based on the size and age of the home and any additional inspections you request (such as sewer, termite or structural inspections). As of 2026, the typical cost for the inspection may be between $400 for a smaller home, and $1,000 for a large, complicated home with multiple extra inspections. While it may feel like an added expense, an inspection can save you thousands of dollars by identifying serious problems before closing.

### Choosing the Right Inspector

Your realtor may recommend inspectors they’ve worked with before, but the choice is ultimately yours. Look for an inspector who is licensed, experienced, and willing to explain their findings in plain language. You should feel comfortable asking questions and attending the inspection if possible. Inspections can last anywhere from 1-3 hours.
