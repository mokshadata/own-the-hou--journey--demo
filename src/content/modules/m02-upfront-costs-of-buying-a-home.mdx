There are some important upfront costs that you will need to plan for when you are buying a home.

* <Term term="down payment" mode="definer">**Down Payment**</Term>: A down payment is the cash you contribute toward the home’s purchase price. Many people think you need 20% down, but some types of loans require much smaller down payments—-sometimes as low as 3%. Learn more in our “[Types of Loans](/own-the-hou--journey--demo/chapters/c05-get-pre-approved-for-a-mortgage/s01-loan-terms/)” section. Down payment assistance, or financial help with your down payment and closing costs, is also available from many different sources in Houston and Harris County. Learn more with our [Down Payment Assistance tool](https://forms.connectivetx.org/340).
* <Term term="earnest money" mode="definer">**Earnest Money**</Term>: This is a portion of your down payment that you put into an <Term term="escrow account">escrow account</Term> during the purchase process. Often, some earnest money is due when you make an offer on a home, and some is due after inspections and appraisals are complete. All earnest money payments will go toward your down payment, so they will reduce the total down payment you need to pay during the closing on your home. However, you will need money available to make these payments before closing.
* <Term term="closing costs" mode="definer">**Closing Costs**</Term>: Fees for the lender, title company, appraisal, inspections, and other costs. Closing costs are typically 2% to 5% of the purchase price. Even if you choose a loan with a low down payment, you also need money to cover these closing costs.
* **Insurance**: You will need to get homeowners insurance before you can close on your home, which will require you to make at least one monthly insurance payment. You may also need to get flood insurance if you are located in an area that is prone to flooding. Learn more about [flood risks in Houston and Harris County here]().
* **Move-in Costs**: Remember to save some money for moving! You may need funds to pay movers, purchase furniture, or make small repairs before you settle in. Don’t use so much of your cash in the purchase process that you have trouble with moving in or paying your first few months of <Term term="mortgage">mortgage</Term> payments.
