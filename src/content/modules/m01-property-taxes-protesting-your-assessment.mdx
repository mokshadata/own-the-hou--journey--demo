Your property tax bill is based on your home’s <Term term="assessed-value" mode="definer">assessed value</Term>, and that value can change from year to year. Each year, the Harris County Appraisal District (HCAD) determines what they believe your home is worth. If your home’s assessed value increases, your property taxes may increase as well. 

In a growing market like the Houston area, rising values are common. However, it’s important to monitor your tax bill and make sure you understand and agree with the assessed value that the appraisal district has assigned to your home. Every year, you’ll receive a notice showing your home’s new assessed value. It’s important to review this notice carefully. Ask yourself:

* Does the value seem reasonable compared to **similar homes** in my neighborhood?
* Are the **property details** (square footage, number of rooms, lot size) accurate?
* Are my exemptions, such as the **homestead exemption**, correctly applied?
* Do I have any **damage** to my home that might decrease its value, compared to what HCAD can see from the outside of the home?

If you believe your home has been overvalued or if any of these details are incorrect, [**you have the right to protest your assessment**]( https://owners.hcad.org/publicArea/InstructionVideo.aspx). Protesting does not mean you’re doing something wrong. It’s a process that allows you to present evidence to HCAD and request a review. 

* The HCAD website allows you to **file a protest online** through the [owners portal](http://owners.hcad.org). Use the *iFile* Number and Account Number from the letter you received with your updated assessed value to allow you to file a protest. 
* You will need **documents** to support your protest. This might include sending examples of similar homes in your neighborhood with lower appraised values, providing documentation of damage to your home like photos or contractor estimates, or providing the deed or survey you received when purchasing your home. 
* HCAD sets a **deadline** each year when protests are due, which will be stated in your updated assessment. Make sure you pay attention to that deadline, because your protest will not be accepted if it arrives late.
* Protesting does not mean that your assessed value and taxes will be lowered automatically. You have to go through the process and potentially appear at a hearing with evidence. However, if something seems off about your assessed value, it’s worth filing a protest; this may help keep your taxes lower this year, and in the future.
