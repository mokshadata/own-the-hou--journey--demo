There’s no single “right” mortgage for everyone. The best loan for you depends on your finances, your goals, and how long you plan to stay in your home. Below are answers to some common questions about mortgage options – you may want to refer back to these as you look for the right lender and get pre-approved.

### What are the main types of mortgage loans?

* <Term client:load term="conventional loans" mode="definer">**Conventional loans**</Term> are common and often offer good rates for buyers with strong credit. They typically require a down payment, and private mortgage insurance (PMI) if you put down less than 20%.
* <Term client:load term="fha loans" mode="definer">**FHA loans**</Term> are designed to be more flexible for first-time buyers and buyers with lower credit scores. They usually have lower down payment requirements and can be easier to qualify for but require mortgage insurance.
* <Term client:load term="va loans" mode="definer">**VA loans**</Term> are available to eligible veterans, service members, and surviving spouses. They often offer very favorable terms, including no down payment.
* <Term client:load term="portfolio loans" mode="definer">**Portfolio loans**</Term> are loans that lenders keep in-house instead of selling to the secondary market. They can be useful for buyers with unique situations, but terms vary widely.

### I’ve heard of “no money down” loans. Do I really not need any money saved upfront?

Not exactly. Some loan programs allow zero down payment, but you’ll still need money for <Term client:load term="closing costs">closing costs</Term>, inspections, appraisals, and move-in expenses. **However, it's important to remember that the less money you put down, the larger <Term client:load term="principal">principal</Term> amount you are borrowing, which means higher costs of borrowing that larger amount.**

### What are builder loans?

Some homebuilders work with preferred lenders and offer special incentives, like covering closing costs or offering lower <Term client:load term="interest rate">interest rates</Term>. These offers can be helpful — but they’re not always the best deal. It’s still important to compare builder loans with other options.

### What’s the difference between a bank and a lender?

Banks, credit unions, and mortgage lenders can all offer home loans. Banks and credit unions usually offer multiple financial services, while mortgage lenders specialize in mortgages only. However, any of these types of lenders could potentially meet your needs. The key is not who they are, but what programs they offer, how clearly they explain terms, and how well they support you through the process. 

### What are ITIN loan programs?

ITIN loans are designed for buyers who don’t have a Social Security number but file taxes using an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN). These loans are not available everywhere and often have different requirements, so working with a knowledgeable lender is essential. You can research which local banks offer ITIN loans online.
