Before you get pre-approved for a mortgage, it helps to understand a few key loan terms. You don’t need to become an expert — but knowing the basics will make it much easier to compare options, ask good questions, and avoid surprises later. Watch our video and read the list of important terms below to learn more.

<Term term="mortgage" mode="definer">**Mortgage**</Term>: A mortgage is a loan you use to buy a home. Most mortgages are paid back over 30 years, though some may be paid back over 15 years or other terms, if you qualify for them. You repay the loan in monthly payments over time; this is your monthly **mortgage payment**.

<Term term="principal" mode="definer">**Principal**</Term>: The principal is the amount of money you borrow to buy your home. Each time you make a mortgage payment, part of it goes toward paying back the principal. As your principal balance goes down, you build equity — the portion of the home you truly own.

**Interest**: Interest is the cost of borrowing money. Your <Term term="interest-rate" mode="definer">**interest rate**</Term> is a percentage that determines how much it will cost you specifically to borrow this principal amount. Even a small increase in interest rate can cause a big increase in your monthly payment. *Watch the video above to see a visual description of how interest rates work.*

<Term term="down-payment" mode="definer">**Down Payment**</Term>: The down payment is the amount you pay upfront towards the purchase price of your home. It’s usually expressed as a percentage of the purchase price. A larger down payment means you borrow less and may qualify for better loan terms — but many first-time buyers buy homes with far less than 20% down (usually between 3%-5%).

<Term term="mortgage-payment" mode="definer">**Mortgage Payment**</Term>: Your monthly payment usually includes:

* Principal payment – this builds your equity
* Interest –  this cost of borrowing is determined by your interest rate
* Property taxes and insurance – these are often collected by your lender and paid through an escrow account to the tax assessor and insurance company directly 
* <Term term="private-mortgage-insurance" mode="definer">**Private mortgage insurance (PMI)**</Term> If you put down less than 20% as a down payment, this cost you pay protects the lender until you build enough equity.

<Term term="debt-to-income-ratio" mode="definer">**Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)**</Term>: Your debt-to-income ratio, or DTI, compares how much money you owe in your personal debts each month, to how much money you earn (pre-tax). Lenders use it to understand whether you can comfortably take on a mortgage payment along with your other debts. A lower DTI usually means you’re more likely to qualify for a lower interest loan and that your mortgage payment will be less of a financial strain. 

<Term term="pre-approval" mode="definer">**Pre-Approval**</Term>: A pre-approval is a lender’s written estimate of how much they may be willing to lend you, based on your financial information. It’s an important step before shopping for a home — and something sellers often expect to see with an offer.

Don’t worry if some of these terms still feel unfamiliar. The next sections of Own the HOU will walk you through how loans work, how to shop for lenders, and how to choose the option that’s right for you.
