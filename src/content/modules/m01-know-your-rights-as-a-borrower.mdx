As you finalize your mortgage, it’s important to remember that you have **rights as a borrower**. The mortgage process can feel complex and intimidating, but laws and regulations are in place to protect you and ensure transparency and fairness.

### You Have the Right to Clear Information

Lenders are required to explain your loan terms in writing. Early in the process, you’ll receive an official <Term client:load term="loan estimate form">**Loan Estimate**</Term> that outlines your interest rate, monthly payment, and expected closing costs for a particular loan. Before closing, you’ll receive an official <Term client:load term="closing disclosure" mode="definer">**Closing Disclosure**</Term> that shows the final terms for the loan. You have the right to review these documents carefully and ask questions if something doesn’t make sense. Plus, you will be asked to sign indicating that you have received them and had a chance to ask questions. 

### You Have the Right to Compare and Ask Questions

You are allowed to compare lenders and loan offers, even late in the process. You also have the right to ask why terms changed, why additional documents are required, or how certain fees are calculated. A lender should be able to explain your loan in plain language.

### You Have the Right to Fair Treatment

Federal fair lending laws prohibit discrimination based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, disability, or receipt of public assistance. If you feel you’re being treated unfairly or pressured into a loan you don’t understand, that’s a signal to pause and seek help.

### You Have Time to Review Before Closing

<u>
By law, **you must receive your Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing**. This gives you time to review the final terms and confirm that they match what you agreed to. You are not required to sign anything you don’t understand.
</u>

### You Have Support - Use It and Ask Questions!

You don’t have to navigate this alone. Your <Term client:load term="housing counselor">housing counselor</Term>, <Term client:load term="lenders">lender</Term>, and <Term client:load term="realtor">realtor</Term> should all be available to help you understand your rights and your loan. If something feels wrong, ask questions! It’s easier to resolve issues before closing than after.
