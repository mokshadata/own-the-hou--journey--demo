Closing day is the final step in buying your home — the day you sign the paperwork, finalize your loan, and officially become a homeowner. It can feel exciting and overwhelming at the same time, but knowing what to expect can help the day go smoothly.

### What Happens on Closing Day

Closing usually takes place at a title company and may last one to two hours. You will need to **bring identification**, like a drivers’ license or passport, to prove your identity. 

You’ll review and sign a series of documents related to your mortgage and ownership of the home. These include:

* Your final loan documents
* The deed transferring ownership
* Paperwork confirming taxes, insurance, and escrow amounts

Your realtor, lender, and the title company will coordinate to make sure everything is ready. Don’t hesitate to pause and ask questions. You are allowed to take the time you need to understand what you’re signing.

### Bringing Your Money to Closing

Before closing day, the title company will tell you exactly how much money you need to bring. This amount is called your <Term term="cash-to-close" mode="definer">cash to close</Term>, and it includes your down payment and any remaining closing costs.

⚠️ Important: You will almost always need to bring this money by **wire transfer or certified check. Wire transfers often need to be sent the day before**. Personal checks, cash, or credit cards are <u>not accepted</u>.

If you are wiring money, protect yourself from fraud by confirming wiring instructions directly with the title company using a trusted phone number. Never rely on emailed instructions alone.

### Final Walkthrough

In many cases, you’ll do a final walkthrough of the home shortly before closing (usually 1-3 days prior). This is your chance to confirm that:

* The home is in the agreed-upon condition
* Any negotiated repairs were completed
* The property is empty and ready for move-in

### The Big Moment: You’re a Homeowner!

Once all documents are signed and funds are received, **the sale is complete**. The title company records the transaction, and you’ll receive the keys to your new home.
