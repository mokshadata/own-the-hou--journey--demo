As you move toward finalizing your mortgage, it’s important to understand the difference between the <Term client:load term="sales price">**sales price**</Term> of the home and your <Term client:load term="loan amount">**loan amount**</Term>. These numbers are related, but they are not the same, and confusing them can lead to surprises at closing.

The <Term client:load term="sales price" mode="definer">**sales price**</Term> is the amount you and the seller agreed on for the home. This is the number written into your offer to purchase the home.

The <Term client:load term="loan amount" mode="definer">**loan amount**</Term> is how much money you are borrowing from your lender. It is usually **less than the sales price** because you are expected to contribute some of your own funds toward the purchase. That difference typically comes from:

* your **<Term client:load term="down payment">down payment</Term>**, and
* any credits or assistance applied to the transaction, such as <Term client:load term="down payment assistance">down payment assistance</Term> or <Term client:load term="credits">seller credits</Term>

*For example, if the home’s sales price is $250,000 and you provide a down payment of $10,000, your loan amount would be $240,000.*

It’s also important to remember that your loan amount is tied to the <Term client:load term="appraised value">**appraised value**</Term> of the home. If the appraisal comes in lower than the sales price, your lender may reduce the loan amount. In that case, you may need to renegotiate the price, bring more cash to closing, or rely on protections in your contract to move forward safely.

<u>
Finally, remember that your monthly mortgage payment is based on your loan amount, not the sales price. A larger down payment means borrowing less which means a lower monthly mortgage payment and less interest paid over time.
</u>
