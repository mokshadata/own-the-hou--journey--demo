As you get closer to closing, you’ll hear the word escrow used a lot. Escrow is not an extra fee or a surprise cost. <Term client:load term="escrow" mode="definer">**Escrow**</Term> simply means that money is being held by a neutral third party, which is usually a title company. The escrow company collects and holds money related to the purchase until the sale is finished and the home officially changes ownership. This ensures that:

* The seller doesn’t receive funds until ownership is transferred
* Your money isn’t released until the sale is complete
* All required payments are made correctly at closing

After your purchase offer was initially accepted, you likely deposited your <Term client:load term="option fee">option fee</Term> and <Term client:load term="earnest money">earnest money</Term> into escrow. A few days before you get to closing, your lender will ask you to deposit additional funds, including:

* The rest of your <Term client:load term="down payment">down payment</Term>, in addition to whatever earnest money you have already deposited
* Funds for <Term client:load term="closing costs">closing costs</Term>, which include fees, commissions, and prepaid items like tax and insurance payments

Once the sale is finalized, the title company distributes the money to the appropriate parties, including the seller, lender, realtors, and local tax authorities.

If something goes wrong before closing and the contract is terminated, the title company  also helps determine who gets what money back from the escrow account, based on the terms of your contract.

**What to Do Before Putting Funds in Escrow**: Before closing, carefully review the amounts listed on your <Term client:load term="closing disclosure">Closing Disclosure</Term> and confirm your wiring instructions by calling the title company. Never send money without verifying details—wire fraud is a real risk, and your team should help you confirm everything safely.
