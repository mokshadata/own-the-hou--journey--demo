Before you can close on your home, you’ll need to get homeowners insurance and provide proof of coverage to your lender. Your loan cannot be finalized without insurance coverage, so it’s important to plan ahead.

### Homeowners Insurance (Required Before Closing)

Homeowners insurance protects your home against damage from things like fire, storms, theft, and certain types of water damage. Your lender requires this coverage because they are providing you a loan against the value of your home. If your home got damaged without insurance, then their loan would be at risk.

Before closing, you will need to:

* Choose an insurance provider. Remember that insurance costs can vary widely, so shopping around and comparing quotes can save you money.
* Select a policy that meets your lender’s requirements. Your lender should tell you what these requirements are.
* Provide proof of coverage showing the policy start date, which is usually the same as the closing date.

The cost of your first year of homeowners insurance is often paid upfront at closing. After that, future premiums are usually included in your monthly mortgage payment and paid through an escrow account with your lender.

### Flood Insurance (may be required)

Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flooding. If your home is in a high-risk flood zone, your lender will require separate flood insurance before closing. Even if it’s not required, flood insurance may still be strongly recommended in parts of Houston and Harris County. 

Remember, you can see whether your home is in or near a floodplain using Harris County’s Flood Education Mapping Tool. Keep in mind that even if you are not in a floodplain, some areas may flood in heavy rainfall or hurricane conditions.

### Other Coverage to Consider

Depending on the home and your situation, you may also want to look into:

* Windstorm or hurricane coverage (required in areas nearer to the coast)
* Additional coverage for valuables, if you will be keeping valuable items at home
* Umbrella liability insurance to reduce liability for any accidents at your property

These may not be required to close, but they can be important for long-term protection.
