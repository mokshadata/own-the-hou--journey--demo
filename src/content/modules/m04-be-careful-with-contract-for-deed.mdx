In Houston, some buyers — especially immigrants and first-time buyers — are offered a <Term term="contract-for-deed" mode="definer">**contract for deed**</Term> instead of a traditional mortgage. These deals are often marketed as an easier path to homeownership, but they can be very risky.

With a contract for deed, you do not get full ownership of the home right away. The seller keeps the title in their name until you’ve paid the full price, which can take many years. During that time, you’re often responsible for repairs, taxes, and insurance, **without the legal protections that come with a mortgage**.

If you miss payments or there’s a dispute, **the seller may be able to cancel the contract and keep both the home and the money you’ve already paid**. In many cases, buyers discover too late that the seller didn’t actually own the property free and clear, or that taxes weren’t being paid.

If someone offers you a contract for deed:

* Pause before signing anything.
* Talk to a housing counselor or legal aid organization. You can sign up to talk to someone through [Own the HOU’s Contact Form](https://ownthehou.org/homebuyers/#connect-with-agency).
* Never rely on verbal promises. Documents for homeownership need to be in writing.

There are safer paths to homeownership. Own the HOU can help you explore legitimate mortgage options and assistance programs that protect your rights.
