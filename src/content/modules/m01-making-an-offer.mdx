Once you’ve found a home you want to buy, the next step is making an offer for purchase. An <Term client:load term="offer" mode="definer">**offer**</Term> is a written proposal to the home seller that explains how much you’re willing to pay for the home — and under what conditions. This section of the toolkit, and the video above, give you an overview of how you make an initial offer. 

Your realtor will help you prepare the offer using a <Term client:load term="standard contract" mode="definer">**standard contract**</Term>. That contract covers much more than just the purchase price. It also includes details about <Term client:load term="closing costs">closing costs</Term>, how long it will take to close, and what happens if certain conditions aren’t met. Before anything is submitted, your realtor should walk you through every section and answer your questions.

When deciding how much to offer for a particular home you are interested in, your realtor will help you analyze:

* Recent sales of similar homes in the area
* The condition of the property
* How long the home has been on the market
* How competitive the market is at that moment

With this information in hand, you will decide on an offer that fits your budget while protecting your interests.

Most offers include <Term client:load term="contingencies" mode="definer">**contingencies**</Term>, which are conditions that must be met for the sale to move forward. Common contingencies relate to inspection timelines, appraisals, and mortgage approval. These protections give you a way to renegotiate or walk away if something doesn’t go as planned.

Once the seller receives your offer, they may accept it, reject it, or come back with a counteroffer. Negotiation is normal, and your realtor will guide you through how to respond.

If the seller rejects your offer, it’s time to move on to the next house and repeat the process. Studies in recent years have shown that it’s not unusual for a first-time homebuyer to submit four or more offers before having an offer accepted. Even if the process is frustrating, stick with your strategy: make sure that you stay within your budget and do your research on every house before making an offer.

The next sections of the toolkit will explain different aspects of your offer in more detail, and the upfront costs you should expect during the offer process.
