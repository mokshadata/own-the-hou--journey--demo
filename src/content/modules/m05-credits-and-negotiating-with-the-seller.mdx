Negotiation doesn’t end once your offer is accepted. In fact, some of the most important negotiations happen after inspections and the appraisal. Understanding how <Term term="credits" mode="definer">**credits**</Term> work — and when to negotiate — can help you protect your budget and avoid unexpected costs.

### What Are Credits?

A credit is money the seller agrees to contribute toward your closing costs or repairs. Instead of fixing an issue before closing, the seller may offer a credit so you can handle the repair yourself after you move in. Credits can also be used to help cover closing costs, which can reduce the amount of cash you need at closing.

Credits are applied at closing and shown on your final settlement documents. They don’t usually reduce your loan amount, but they can make the purchase more affordable upfront.

### When Negotiations Happen

Negotiations often happen:

* After a home inspection, if issues are discovered
* After an appraisal, if the value comes in lower than expected

Your realtor will help you review the findings and decide what’s reasonable to request. Not every issue needs to be fixed, but significant safety concerns, expensive repairs, or a low appraisal are common points of negotiation.

### How to Approach Negotiation

Negotiation is normal and expected. The goal is not to “win,” but to reach an agreement that works for both you and the seller.

Your realtor can help you:

* Decide whether to ask for repairs or credits
* Determine which issues matter most
* Understand how the market affects your ability to negotiate

In competitive markets, sellers may be less willing to negotiate. In slower markets, buyers often have more flexibility. Knowing where you stand — and what protections are in your contract if you want to walk away — helps you make informed decisions.
