Once you’re under contract and your lender has your application, your loan moves into the approval process. This stage can feel slow or confusing, but it’s a normal and necessary part of finalizing your mortgage. The process usually has two main phases: processing and underwriting. 

### Processing

During <Term term="processing" mode="definer">**processing**</Term>, your lender gathers and reviews all the documents needed to support your loan application. This includes things like pay stubs, tax returns, bank statements, and verification of your employment. The processor’s job is to make sure your file is complete and accurate before it goes to underwriting.

At this stage, your lender may ask for additional documents or clarification, even if you already provided similar information earlier. This can feel repetitive, but it’s common. Lenders often need updated documents in order to show that all of your information is still current and accurate.

### Underwriting

Once processing is complete, your file goes to <Term term="underwriting" mode="definer">**underwriting**</Term>. The underwriter is responsible for deciding whether the loan meets all program requirements and lender guidelines. The underwriter reviews:

* Your income, including whether you have had steady employment and income recently
* Your credit history
* Your debts
* Your assistance programs, to make sure you are eligible to receive the assistance
* The appraisal of the home

You may receive a **conditional approval**, which means your loan is on track but requires a few final items. These might include updated bank statements, explanations for recent transactions, or proof that certain conditions have been met.

### Avoiding Financial Changes During Loan Approval

<u>
It’s important to avoid major financial changes during this time. Opening new credit accounts, changing jobs, or making large purchases can delay or jeopardize approval.
</u>

If everything checks out, the lender will issue a final approval, and you’ll be cleared to close.
