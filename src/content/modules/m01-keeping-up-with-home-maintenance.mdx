Regular maintenance is an important way to protect your home from damage. 

### Keep Up With Preventative Maintenance

Small tasks done consistently can prevent bigger, more expensive problems later. Think of your home like a car. Regular oil changes and inspections help avoid breakdowns. The same is true for your house. Catching issues  like a small leak or clogged gutter early can save thousands of dollars in future repairs. The list below provides some guidance on regular maintenance tasks.

### Every 3–6 Months

* Replace filters in your heating and air conditioning (HVAC) system
* Test smoke and carbon monoxide detectors
* Check for leaks under sinks and around toilets

### Twice a Year

* Clean your gutters and downspouts
* Inspect your roof for visible damage
* Trim tree branches away from the house

### Once a Year

* Have a professional service your HVAC system
* Check the seals, or caulking, around windows and doors
* Inspect your exterior siding and paint for any signs of damage, leaks, or pests

### Budget for Larger Repairs

While preventative maintenance helps, you’ll still need to budget for larger repairs. For example, appliances like your washer, dryer, dishwasher, and stove will likely need to be replaced every 10-15 years. Your roof will likely need replacement every 15-20 years. 

A common rule of thumb is to set aside about **2% of your home’s value** each year for maintenance and repairs in a savings account. While you may not spend this amount every year, budgeting for these expenses will help you handle these issues quickly and avoid financial problems or further damage. 

If you ever feel overwhelmed by needed repairs, don’t ignore the problem. Explore [repair assistance programs](/chapters/c08-you-re-a-homeowner-protect-your-home/s07-help-to-keep-your-home/m01-help-to-keep-your-home/) early. Help is often available before small issues become major ones.
