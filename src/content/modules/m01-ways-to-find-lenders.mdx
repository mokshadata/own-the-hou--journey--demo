Once you understand the basics of how mortgages work, the next step is learning how to shop for a loan and choose a lender. Just like homes, mortgage loans can vary widely — and comparing options can save you money over the life of your loan.

Shopping for a mortgage means comparing interest rates, fees, and loan features from more than one lender. Even a small difference in interest rate can add up to thousands of dollars over time.

There are three main ways to shop for home loans:

* **Work with a mortgage broker.** A <Term term="mortgage-broker" mode="definer">**mortgage broker**</Term> doesn’t lend money directly. Instead, they help connect you with different lenders and loan products so you can compare multiple options, without having to do all the work of reaching out to lenders yourself. Brokers can be helpful if you want support comparing multiple options or if your financial situation is complicated. Mortgage brokers take a commission for their services, so you should ask them to clearly explain how they are paid, and should feel comfortable asking questions before moving forward. If you are looking to use Down Payment Assistance or other financing, ask whether a mortgage broker is familiar with the specific programs you are interested in before moving forward.
* **Shop with local lenders like traditional banks, community banks, and credit unions.** Some local lenders offer special programs, flexible underwriting, or “<Term term="portfolio-loans" mode="definer">**portfolio loans**</Term>” that may come with more flexibility and even closing cost assistance. Your housing counselor or realtor can help you understand some of your local options, or you can work with a mortgage broker to find some of these. 
* **Shop with a mortgage lender that specializes in mortgages only.** There are local and nationwide mortgage lenders that specialize in offering mortgages and don’t offer other types of banking services. Some of them have easy online application processes that can be attractive if you’re looking for a quick way to understand whether you qualify for a loan. Your mortgage broker or realtor can offer guidance on lenders that may offer competitive rates in your local market.
