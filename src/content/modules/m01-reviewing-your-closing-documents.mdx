Before closing day, you’ll receive several important documents that explain the final terms of your home purchase and mortgage. Reviewing these documents carefully is one of the most important steps you’ll take. The video above breaks down what to look for in your closing documents and walks through the key sections step by step.

The most important document is the Closing Disclosure. This form shows your final loan terms, interest rate, monthly payment, and all the costs you’ll pay at closing. By law, you must receive your Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. This gives you time to review it and ask questions.

As you review your Closing Disclosure, pay close attention to the following items:

* Your loan amount and interest rate
* Your total monthly payment, including taxes and insurance
* Your cash to close
* Any credits or adjustments from the seller

Sometimes the numbers on your Closing Disclosure can look different from earlier estimates, but changes should make sense and be clearly explained. If something looks wrong or different than what you expected, speak up right away! This is the best time to resolve issues.

Besides your Closing Disclosure, you’ll also review other documents related to ownership and financing, including:

* The promissory note: This document is your promise to the lender to repay your mortgage loan. It should have the same loan amount, interest rate, and monthly payments as your Closing Disclosure.
* The deed to the home. This is the document that will certify your ownership of the home once you have closed. 

You don’t need to understand every legal term, but you should understand the big picture: what you’re borrowing, what you’re paying, and what you’re agreeing to.
