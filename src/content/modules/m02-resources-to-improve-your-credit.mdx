There are many resources available to improve your credit, including non-profit credit repair agencies that provide counseling and classes to help improve your credit. Alliance Financial Ministries provides classes and counseling in the Houston area. You can also look up a local certified credit counselor at the National Foundation for Credit Counseling website. If you are concerned about your debts or unsure how to improve credit situation, consulting a counselor could be a great first step.

There are some general principles you can follow to improve your credit: 
* **Pay down debts.**
  
  Generally, credit card debts and payday loans have higher interest rates. Paying high-interest loans off first may help you reduce debts. A financial or credit counselor can help you decide which debts to pay down first, or potentially consolidate debts in order to get a lower interest rate. 

* **Make on-time payments.**

  Make all credit card and loan payments by the due date. Set reminders to ensure you can pay on time each month.

* **Dispute any errors on your credit report.**

  Access your credit report at annualcreditreport.com to see if there are any errors. Sometimes, you might have debts for someone with a similar name reported on your credit report, or your credit report might contain debts you have already paid off. If you find errors, the U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau provides resources to help remove these from your credit report.

* **Don’t add new lines of credit.**

  Avoid taking out new credit cards or loans while you are working to buy a home. Each “hard” inquiry on your credit report, which is required for a new card or loan, can have a negative impact on your credit score. The only exception to this rule is if you need to establish credit (see the next point below!). 

* **Establish credit.**

  If you like to pay in cash rather than use credit cards or take loans out, that can be a great financial principle. However, if you don’t have any credit cards or loans at all, you also don’t have a history of paying off debts on time. This means your credit score could be low even though you have good financial health. If this is an issue for you, consider getting a no-fee credit card. You can limit your use of this card to minor purchases and pay it off on time each month, and this will help build your credit score.
