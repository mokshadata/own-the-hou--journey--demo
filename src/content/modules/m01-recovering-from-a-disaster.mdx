If your home is damaged in a disaster, you need to act quickly, but carefully, to begin your recovery. 

**Prioritize your safety first**. Avoid standing water and downed power lines, and don’t go into your home if it looks unsafe or has structural damage. 

**Document damage and contact your insurance provider(s)**. Once you are able to access your home, it’s important to take the following steps for insurance purposes:

* Document the damage with photos and videos **<i>before</i>** you start to clean up.
* Contact your insurance provider(s) as soon as possible to begin a claim. This includes your homeowners insurance provider, and your flood or windstorm insurance providers if you have these policies.

**Apply for assistance**. If you need help with affording an alternate place to stay, paying for home repairs, or affording other resources like food and medication, you can:

* [Fill out the Connective Survey.](https://www.connectivetx.org/what-we-do/storm-needs-survey/) This survey documents community needs and connects people to resources, including home repair resources, as they become available.
* [Call 211.](https://www.211.org/) This United Way helpline can provide connections to immediate resources like food and shelter.
* [Apply for FEMA assistance.](https://www.fema.gov/assistance/individual) FEMA, a federal agency, has programs that help reimburse you for post-disaster expenses, and can provide resources for long-term housing if your home needs extensive repairs before you can return.

### Protect your home with these tips.

* Avoid <Term term="contractor-fraud" mode="definer">contractor fraud</Term>, which happens when a contractor takes money for repairs and fails to complete the job. Be cautious of contractors offering fast repairs. Verify licenses and avoid paying large upfront deposits. 
* <u>Don’t stop paying your mortgage</u> without contacting your <Term term="mortgage-servicer" mode="definer">mortgage servicer</Term>. If you have trouble making payments, call your servicer and ask for your mortgage to be placed in forbearance. This gives you a time period when you don’t have to make payments or you can make reduced payments. Make sure you understand how long your loan will be in <Term term="forbearance" mode="definer">forbearance</Term>, and what you will owe at the end of the forbearance period.
