Realtors are typically paid through a <Term term="commission" mode="definer">**commission**</Term> connected to the sale of a home. Before you begin touring homes, you’ll usually sign a written agreement with your realtor (sometimes called a <Term term="buyer representation agreement" mode="definer">**buyer representation agreement**</Term>). This outlines the services your realtor will provide, the timeframe for working together, and how the realtor will be paid. 

Here are some things to know about the buyer representation agreement and its importance:

* Before 2024, the total commission on a home and the percentage provided to the buyer’s and seller’s agents was standardized (usually 6% total, with 3% for the buyer’s agent and 3% for the seller’s agent). However, compensation is now fully negotiable. There’s no longer a “standard” split.

* This means that your realtor’s pay must be discussed upfront. Your buyer representation agreement explains how your realtor will be compensated, so it’s important to read those details and ask questions where necessary.

* **When you make an offer on a home, you have to specify who will pay your realtor’s commission.** In the past, the seller usually covered the commission for the buyer’s agent. Now, this can depend on what’s written into your offer and negotiated during the sale. Remember to discuss this question with your realtor when you are making an offer.
