<Term term="credit score" mode="definer">**Credit scores**</Term> are a tool that banks use to understand how likely a particular person is to pay back their loans or debts successfully, which means they are an important part of your mortgage application. However, they are not a perfect tool for understanding your financial situation, because they primarily look at how well you have successfully paid off debts and loans in the past. If you are someone who doesn’t like to take on debt, or prefers cash to credit cards, you could have a low credit score even though you pay all of your bills up front.

This section provides a short overview of credit scores, but if you would like more information on credit scores and how they are calculated, you can go to [this lesson from the National Foundation for Credit Counseling](https://www.nfcc.org/understanding-your-credit-score-course/).

Some of the factors that are used to calculate your credit score include:
* **Payment history**: Your history of making on-time payments on accounts such as credit cards, home loans, and car loans.
* **Amounts owed**: How much you currently owe in total debt.
* **Length of credit history**: How long you have had specific accounts open for, such as your credit card, home loan, or car loan.
* **Mix of accounts**: How many different types of credit accounts you have (installment vs revolving).
* **Inquiries for new credit**: How many times you have tried to open new credit accounts. 

There are three main “credit bureaus” that specialize in calculating credit scores, called <u>*Experian*</u>, <u>*TransUnion*</u>, and <u>*Equifax*</u>. These three bureaus may have slightly different scores for you, but they should all be in the same general range.

The federal government allows you to request free reports on an annual basis from all three agencies by going to [annualcreditreport.com](https://annualcreditreport.com).  Once you have your report(s), you can compare the number to the following ranges:

| | Range |
|--|------|
| Exceptional | 800+ |
| Very good | 740 to 799 |
| Good | 670 to 739 |
| Fair | 580 to 669 |
| Poor | 579 and below |


If you find that your credit score is low, you’re not alone! Low credit scores affect all kinds of people in many different situations – and many successful homebuyers start with some credit challenges.
