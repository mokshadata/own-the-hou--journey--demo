Estate planning is one of the most important steps you can take as a homeowner.If you pass away without clear legal arrangements in place, your home does not automatically go to the person you intended to leave it to. Instead, it may become what’s known as **heirship property**, which can create serious complications for your family.

### Wills and Transfer on Death Deeds (TODDs)

Wills and Transfer on Death Deeds (TODDs) are two common tools homeowners use for estate planning related to their homes. Here are some of the key differences between these tools.

* A **will** is a legal document that allows you to state who should inherit your property. However, even with a will in place, your home must usually go through probate court. Probate can be time-consuming and expensive, and it may take months before your family can legally transfer ownership.
* A **TODD** document allows you to name the person or people who will automatically inherit your property when you pass away, without going through probate. The TODD must be <u>signed, notarized, and recorded</u> with Harris County (or the county where your property is located) before your death. Using a TODD to pass your property down can make a significant financial difference. 

### What Happens Without a Plan?

If no will or TODD exists, your property may become heirship property, meaning it passes to multiple heirs under Texas law. Over time, ownership can become split among many relatives.

This makes it difficult to:

* Sell the home
* Refinance the mortgage
* Qualify for repair assistance 
* Make major decisions about the property

Resolving heirship issues can require court proceedings and substantial legal costs.

### Take Action Now

Planning ahead protects your family from confusion, delays, and financial issues. The Earl Carl Institute (ECI) at the Thurgood Marshall School of Law, an Own the HOU member organization, has created a [step-by-step guide to filling out and recording a TODD](https://www.tsulaw.edu/centers/ECI/Transfer-on-Death-Deed-Instructions-2023.pdf) in Harris County.
