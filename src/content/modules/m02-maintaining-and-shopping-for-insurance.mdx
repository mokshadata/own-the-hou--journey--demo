Your insurance coverage is one of the most important tools you have to protect your home. But insurance isn’t something you set up once and forget. Rates, risks, and coverage needs usually change over time. To keep your insurance affordable, you should review your coverage and consider shopping for new policies each year.

### Review Your Homeowners Coverage Every Year

At least once a year, take time to review your homeowners insurance policy. Ask yourself:

* Does my coverage reflect the **current value** of my home?
* Have I done **renovations or improvements** that should be included in the policy?
* Do I understand **what is <u>not</u> covered**?

### Understand Flood Insurance and Your Home’s Needs

Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage. Flood insurance must be purchased separately, often through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or a private insurer.

If your home is in a high-risk flood zone, your lender will require flood insurance. But even if it’s not required, many Houston homeowners choose to carry it. Flooding can occur outside mapped floodplains, and just a few inches of water can cause significant damage to your home. Some policies have a waiting period before coverage begins, so don’t wait until a storm is forecasted to explore your flood insurance options.

### Compare Insurance Policy Costs Each Year

Insurance premiums in the Houston and Harris County area have increased in recent years. To keep your costs affordable, it can be useful to use an online tool or work with an <Term term="insurance-agent" mode="definer">insurance agent</Term> to compare quotes from multiple providers. When you’re comparing quotes, make sure to review things like:

* **Deductibles**. Your <Term term="deductible" mode="definer">deductible</Term> describes how much you will have to pay out of your own pocket before your insurance coverage kicks in.
* **Coverage limits**. “Coverage” describes the total amount that the insurance company will pay if your home is damaged, and what items are covered. Usually, policies describe how much they will pay for repairs and replacement of your home itself, and how much they will pay for the contents of your home, like your furniture, technology, and other belongings. Sometimes a lower price is associated with less coverage, or excludes things you would like to have covered. Your mortgage servicer will also have minimum standards for insurance coverage, so make sure to check any new policies with them before signing up.

Remember, while it’s important to shop around, the lowest premium isn’t always the best option. Choose a policy that offers enough coverage for you to manage the risks of damage and that meets your lender’s requirements. If you don’t understand your policy, ask questions. An insurance agent should be able to explain your coverage in plain language and help you make informed decisions.
