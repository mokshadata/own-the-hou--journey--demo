Once you have selected a lender to work with, it’s time to think about getting pre-approved – which is one of the most important steps in the homebuying process. A pre-approval is a lender’s written estimate of how much they may be willing to lend you, based on a review of your financial information. It helps you understand your price range, shows sellers that you’re a serious buyer, and can make your offer more competitive.

### What a Pre-Approval Means

When you apply for pre-approval, a lender looks at your income, debts, credit history, and savings. They use this information to estimate the loan amount you may qualify for and the type of loan that could work for you. A pre-approval is usually valid for a limited time (often 60–90 days), and it may include conditions you’ll need to meet before final loan approval. 

While a pre-approval is not a guarantee, it gives you a strong starting point and helps you shop for homes with confidence.

### Pre-Approval vs. Pre-Qualification

These two terms sound similar, but they are not the same.

* A pre-qualification is usually a quick, informal estimate based on information you provide yourself. It can be helpful early on, but it doesn’t carry much weight with sellers.
* A pre-approval is more detailed and requires documentation. Because the lender has verified your financial information, sellers take pre-approvals much more seriously — and many won’t consider an offer without one.
