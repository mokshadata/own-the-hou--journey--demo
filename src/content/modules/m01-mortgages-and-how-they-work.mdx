Most people buy homes with a <Term term="mortgage" mode="definer">**mortgage**</Term>--a loan from a bank or lender that you pay back over time, usually 15 to 30 years. Your monthly mortgage payment typically includes:

* <Term term="principal" mode="definer">**Principal**</Term>: The amount you borrow to buy your home. Each time you make a mortgage payment, part of it goes toward chipping away at that debt. As you pay down the principal, you build <Term term="equity" mode="definer">**equity**</Term>--the portion of the home that you truly own.
* <Term term="interest" mode="definer">**Interest**</Term>: The lender’s fee for letting you borrow money. In the early years of your loan, a larger share of each payment goes toward interest. * **Taxes and Insurance**: Property taxes and insurance costs are usually added to your monthly payment and held in an <Term term="escrow account" mode="definer">**escrow account**</Term> by your lender. This portion of your payments is likely to increase over time, so you should plan up front to have some extra room in your budget for future years to cover increased monthly payments.
