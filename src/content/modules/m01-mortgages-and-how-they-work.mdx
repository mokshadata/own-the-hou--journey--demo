Most people buy homes with a <Term client:load term="mortgage" mode="definer">**mortgage**</Term>--<span class="term--inline-definition" data-term="mortgage">a loan from a bank or lender that you pay back over time, usually 15 or 30 years</span>. Your monthly mortgage payment typically includes:

* <Term client:load term="principal" mode="definer">**Principal**</Term>: The amount you borrow to purchase your home. Each time you make a mortgage payment, part of it goes toward *chipping away at that debt*. As you pay down the principal, you build <Term client:load term="equity" mode="definer">**equity**</Term>--the portion of the home that you truly own.
* <Term client:load term="interest" mode="definer">**Interest**</Term>: The lender’s fee for letting you borrow money. In the early years of your loan, a larger share of each payment goes toward interest. Over time, more of your payment goes toward principal, helping you build equity faster. Your interest rate will affect the total monthly payment amount
* **Taxes and Insurance**: Property taxes and insurance costs are usually added to your monthly payment and held in an <Term client:load term="escrow account" mode="definer">**escrow account**</Term> by your lender. This portion of your payments is likely to increase over time, so you should plan up front to have some extra room in your budget for future years to cover increased monthly payments.
 Learn how to keep these payments from increasing too much in [Section 8](/chapters/c08-you-re-a-homeowner-protect-your-home/s02-maintaining-and-shopping-for-insurance/m01-maintaining-and-shopping-for-insurance/).

Your <u>_interest rate_</u>, <u>_down payment_</u>, <u>_loan type_</u>, and <u>_insurance_</u> and <u>_tax costs_</u> all affect your monthly payment.
