Buying a home comes with big decisions—how much house you will purchase, which loan works best for you and what assistance programs you’ll apply for. That’s where a <Term term="housing-counselor" mode="definer">**housing counselor**</Term> can be one of your most valuable teammates.
 
A “counselor” may sound like someone you only need when you’re having trouble  – but that’s not true for Housing Counselors. <span class="term--inline-definition" data-term="housing-counselor">They are homeownership experts who can help you understand the process from start to finish.  They can review your credit, help you build a realistic budget, explain loan options in plain language, and help you create an action plan that fits your goals.</span> If you’re already pretty far along, they can still be a valuable resource in helping you troubleshoot specific homebuying questions—like how to strengthen your mortgage application or how exactly down payment assistance works.

One of the best parts? Housing counselors are neutral resources who don’t get paid based on whether you buy a home or not. Their job is to help you make informed decisions and move forward with confidence.

Through Own the HOU, you can connect with three trusted non-profit organizations that offer HUD-certified homebuyer education and housing counseling: [Avenue CDC](https://avenuecdc.org/homeownership-center/homebuyers-education-class/), [Fifth Ward CRC](https://www.fifthwardcrc.org/programs-services/homeownership-promotion-preservation/), and [Tejano Center for Community Concerns](https://ryssorg-40-us-central1-01.preview.finalsitecdn.com/programs-resources/supportive-housing/affordable-housing-and-community-redevelopment/housing-counseling). These agencies provide homebuyer education classes and one-on-one counseling sessions, and some offer additional opportunities like lunch-and-learns and workshops to help you stay informed throughout your homebuying journey.
