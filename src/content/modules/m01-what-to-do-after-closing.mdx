Again, Own the HOU wishes you congratulations — you’re officially a homeowner! 🎉 Buying a home is a long process, and it’s hard to think about dealing with even more documents and processes after closing. But before you fully settle in, there are a few important steps to take to protect your investment, lower your costs, and make sure your ownership records are in order.

In the first few weeks after closing, you’ll want to:

* Make sure your <Term term="homestead exemption" mode="definer">homestead exemption</Term> is filed. The homestead exemption is a tax reduction just for homeowners (stating that this is your primary residence), and it can save you <u>thousands of dollars</u> in property taxes every year. You can also file other exemptions if you are over 65, disabled, or a disabled veteran. Learn more about exemptions and how to file at the [Harris County Appraisal District website](https://hcad.org/hcad-help/texas-first-time-property-owner/exemptions/property-tax-exemptions-for-homeowners/).
* **Update your address** with important parties, like your bank or your workplace.
* Make sure all of your **utility accounts**, like your electric bill and water bill, have been set up in your name in your new home.
* **Organize and safely store your closing documents.** You’ll want to have your deed and other closing documents available if you need them to prove your ownership in the future, so scan and store digital copies, and keep the originals stored safely on a high shelf. You may want to include these documents with your **emergency evacuation kit** in the event that you need to evacuate during a hurricane or other natural disaster.

You should also consider your long-term and **estate planning** at this time, because it’s important to be clear about what happens to your home in the event that you pass away. 

* If you want to leave the property to a family member or members, consider filling out and recording a <Term term="transfer on death deed" mode="definer">Transfer on Death Deed (TODD)</Term> with Harris County (or the county where your property is located) as a way to transfer the property legally and allow your family to avoid probate costs and headaches after your death. 
* Probate can be lengthy and expensive. When it’s time for your family to inherit your home, a TODD can make the difference between $3,500 in probate costs for a lawyer, versus just $35 in document filing costs.
* The Earl Carl Institute (ECI) at the Thurgood Marshall School of Law has created a [step-by-step guide to filling out and recording a TODD](https://www.tsulaw.edu/centers/ECI/Transfer-on-Death-Deed-Instructions-2023.pdf) that you can use to complete this process. ECI is an Own the HOU member organization.
