The lender for your mortgage will do a thorough review of your income, debts, credit score, and other factors in order to calculate your "**<Term term="pre-approval amount" mode="definer">pre-approval amount</Term>**", or the total loan value you can be approved for. This loan value is based on factors including your income, your debts, and your credit score.

To learn more about how to choose a lender, visit Section X.

To learn more about how to get a pre-approval and see a checklist of documents you will need to bring to your lender, visit Section Y.

When you receive a pre-approval, it is important to remember a few things:

* **Your Pre-Approval May be Top Dollar**: Pre-approvals often reflect a “stretch” budget – meaning they are the maximum amount that you could possibly take out in a home loan. But your maximum pre-approval amount may not match your desired budget for a home. 
* **Consider Other Financial Goals**: If you are saving for college, planning to start a family, or want to keep your housing expenses low so you can have more disposable income, you should look at your monthly budget and decide what you want your monthly payment to be.
